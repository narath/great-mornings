Great Mornings

What makes for a great morning?

A short recitation for beginning the day. Say it out loud.

A suggested daily recitation

We wake, and refresh ourselves.We center ourselves with deep breaths, with calmness,with solidity in being in our bodies,in the remembrance of our strengths.

I look forward — to the challenges ahead for which I have been preparing myself.I look forward — to being useful and helpful to my family, my friends, my community and our earth.

I am grateful for all that I have received from my loved ones, from my community, from the earth and from the work and effort that I have done.

I welcome the chance to learn and improve.I am positive and proactive.I give respect, and I expect respect.I am an independent thinker.I use reason and science to see and understand more clearly.I use humanism to guide my actions and my decisions.I have reverence for all life. I am an earthling.

I am grateful for the food that we have prepared. It nourishes me and gives me energy.I know there are many who do not have this. I am aware and will do what I can to help them.

I find strength and support in this family who loves me, and who wishes the best for me.I wish the best for them, and will give them my strength and support through my positive, loving actions.

I welcome the day.I am ready.

Go well.

Make it yours

The words are mine, and they are yours to change. Download the Word version and edit it like any other document; or fork the repository and rewrite the text; or paste your own straight into the app, which keeps a library of your own recitations, privately, on your phone.

There is a blank version with the shape and none of the words, if that is easier than starting from mine. I am not collecting other people's versions — write the one that is true for you and say that instead.

Who wrote this

I'm Narath Carlile. I wrote this recitation for my own family — we said it at breakfast, the kids half awake, me holding coffee — and over a few years it became the thing that set the tone of the day. It isn't a prayer and it isn't a productivity trick. The mornings I said it went better than the mornings I didn't. That's the whole claim.

The same work that produced it produced MakeTimeFlow, which is what I build: a way of pointing finite attention at the things that actually matter. Great Mornings is free and always will be — it's the part of that work that belongs to everyone.
